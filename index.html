How you get paid

On a subscription client, you earn 25% of what’s left after Stripe fees in the first month. After that it drops to 20% a month, to maintain the client, and runs for as long as you retain them as project manager. On one-time packages you earn 20% of the sale.

Sell

Step 2

25%

Step 3

Then 20% a month

Every month after the first, to maintain the client, for as long as the client stays and you retain them.

What keeps it rolling

  • The month finishes and the client rolls over into the next month.
  • You stay on as that client’s project manager and step in when something is out of the ordinary.
  • If a client is reassigned to you, you get that month’s commission prorated from when they were assigned to you.

That’s why managing clients in Slack matters as much as closing them.

Who gets the commission when two reps touch one lead

The second rep gets it, if…

A rep reached out to a lead but:

  • hadn’t officially made contact, or
  • the lead was dropped, or
  • the rep wasn’t able to sell to them,

…and another rep picks up the account and closes it. The commission goes to the second rep.

The 3-day rule

No rep can reach out to a lead unless both are true:

  • It’s been 3 days since the last activity on that lead.
  • No follow-up note or meeting is in place to continue the conversation.

Before you contact any lead: it must be 3 days since the last activity and there must be no follow-up note or meeting in place. Otherwise leave it alone. Why this matters for commission

The prompts below fill it in for you. It stays in your own browser only — it is not sent anywhere. If you skip this, replace the placeholder yourself.

List the leads whose last activity was more than 3 days ago and where no follow-up note or meeting is in place to continue the conversation. Oldest activity first, with name, phone, email and the latest note. Show which sheet row each lead came from.

Tips that save you a bad call

  • Share the sheet so Claude can read it. If Claude says it can’t open the link, check the sheet’s sharing settings or ask the owner.
  • Always ask for the row. Each lead should come with the sheet row it came from, so you can check it.
  • Use prompt 7 for leads you don’t already own. It follows the 3-day rule. Your own follow-ups (a call back you promised) are yours to continue.
  • Re-check the sheet before you call. Claude is a fast first pass, not the source of truth.
  • Never paste client passwords or payment details into Claude.

Claude doesn’t update the sheet

Claude only helps you read the sheet and build a list. The sheet is updated from the notes you post in your Slack channel, which are mapped into the right row. So the list is only as good as the notes you log.

Logging notes (critical)

Every rep has a dedicated Slack channel for updating notes to the live sheet. Our AI reads each note and matches it to the right row. Leave out the timestamp, the name or the contact detail and the note can’t be matched to a row.

Required

Timestamp

Date and time of the call or message.

Required

Lead / client name

Full name, plus the business if you have it.

Required

Phone OR email

At least one. This is how the AI finds the row.

Strict one-line template — post in #[your-rep-channel]
[YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM] | [lead name, business] | [phone OR email] | [what happened] | [next step + date]

The first three fields are required. The last two aren’t part of the required list, but add them — they’re what makes a note useful the next time you build a calling list. One lead per line.

Managing clients in Slack Draft

Each closed client gets their own Slack channel. The editor and the client talk to each other directly there. You, as project manager, step in when something is out of the ordinary. The escalation chain below is set by the owner; the details around it are a draft proposal.

  1. 1

    One channel per client

    Name it with the business, for example #client-businessname. Everything about that client lives there.

  2. 2

    Post the hand-off, then pin the basics

    When a client closes, a new client channel is set up and you post the hand-off. Then pin one message with: the package, start date, brand notes, file-drop link, client contact, and who the project manager is.

  3. 3

    Editor and client work directly

    One thread per video request, with the raw-footage link, references and due date in the first message. Revision rounds stay in the same thread.

  4. 4

    Deliveries go in the thread, marked ✅

    The editor posts the finished video as a reply in the request’s thread and adds a ✅.

  5. 5

    You step in when it’s out of the ordinary

    Don’t get between the editor and client on routine work. Do jump in when you’re tagged, or when you notice something off. See the escalation chain.

  6. 6

    Weekly check-in from you

    A short weekly post is your early-warning system: it’s how you spot a problem before the client brings it up.

Package notes

What changes in the channel depending on the package.

Single Edit

One video request. 2 rounds of revisions — track them in the thread so everyone knows which round it is.

1-Week Sprint

7-day window. Pin the start date. The client picks one format: 5 simple short-form, 2–3 complex short-form, or 1 long-form. No auto-renewal.

Unlimited

Rolling queue. As soon as one edit is approved, the next one starts. Keep the queue order clear in the channel.

Precautions: spotting a client who may leave Draft

A client who leaves ends the commission, so catch the signs early. Everyone in the channel has a part. On Unlimited a client can cancel up to 15 days before the next billing cycle, so a hint of leaving is time-sensitive.

Warning signs — tag the project manager if you see any

  • The client mentions cancelling, pausing or leaving.
  • The same video keeps going back for revisions.
  • The client’s tone turns frustrated, or they say it isn’t what they asked for.
  • Replies slow down, or the channel goes quiet.
  • Requests stop coming in (an empty queue on Unlimited).
  • A delivery is running past its window (short-form 1–3 days, long-form 3–7 days).

Clients

Tell them at onboarding, and put it in the pinned message.

  • If anything feels off, @ the project manager in the channel. No need to wait.
  • If you’re thinking of leaving, say so to the project manager first so they can help.

Editors

You talk to the client most, so you see problems first.

  • Tag the project manager as soon as you notice a warning sign. Early is better than late.
  • Don’t debate scope, pricing or cancelling with the client. Hand it over.
  • If you can’t hit a delivery window, say so and tag the project manager.

Project managers

You’re the first stop.

  • Acknowledge in the thread right away so the client knows someone has it.
  • Read the whole thread and check the package terms (revision rounds, delivery windows).
  • Fix it if it’s within scope. If it’s out of scope or the client plans to leave, @ Mohamed Nour.
  • Never promise refunds, discounts or exceptions yourself.
Client can see the channel? Keep the thread calm and short. Move sensitive details (price, cancelling, who’s at fault) to a DM or internal channel with the project manager and Mohamed Nour.

Rules of the road

Simple rules that keep clients, the owner and your commission safe.

Do

  • Quote the packages exactly as written in the packages section.
  • Tell prospects to book a meeting first to understand the package before they buy.
  • Send the payment link that matches the package they chose.
  • Check the 3-day rule before you contact a lead: 3 days since the last activity, and no follow-up note or meeting in place.
  • Post the hand-off in the new client channel as soon as a client closes, with correct contact details.
  • Log every update in your Slack channel — timestamp, name, phone or email.
  • Let the editor and client talk directly. Step in only when something is out of the ordinary.
  • Escalate in order: @ project manager first, then @ Mohamed Nour if it’s out of scope.
  • Flag a client who might leave right away. Don’t wait.

Don’t

  • Promise results, guarantees or delivery times that aren’t listed.
  • Offer discounts or quote a price that isn’t on this page.
  • Promise “same editor” on Single Edit or Sprint. It’s only listed on Unlimited.
  • Promise refunds, discounts or exceptions to keep a client. Hand it to Mohamed Nour.
  • Jump into routine editor–client threads, or skip the project manager and go straight to Mohamed Nour.
  • Call Unlimited output a guarantee. 12–30 short-form or 4–8 long-form is an average.
  • Paste client passwords or payment details into Claude, Slack or anywhere else.
  • Skip the note after a call — no note, no sheet update.
  • Contact a lead with activity in the last 3 days, or one that has a follow-up note or meeting in place.
  • Share this page outside the team. It contains commission terms.

FAQ

Who do I ask if I don’t know the answer?
In a client channel, tag the project manager. If it’s out of scope, the project manager tags Mohamed Nour. For anything outside a client channel, book time on the owner’s calendar. Don’t guess with a prospect.

Editor or client: tag the project manager. Project manager: fix it if it’s within scope; if it’s out of scope, tag Mohamed Nour. See the escalation chain.
A client says they’re thinking of leaving. What do I do?
Acknowledge it right away, read the thread, and don’t promise refunds, discounts or exceptions. What’s the difference between short-form and long-form?
Short-form is 90 seconds or under. Long-form is over 90 seconds. Short-form is delivered in 1–3 days; long-form in 3–7 days.
Does Claude update the lead sheet for me?
No. Claude helps you read the sheet and build a calling list. The sheet is updated from the notes you post in your Slack channel, which map into the correct row.
A prospect wants to buy right now. Can I just send the link?

Which payment link do I send?
The one for the package they chose: Single Edit ($149), 1-Week Sprint ($299) or Unlimited ($999/month). All three links are in the packages section with copy buttons.
What if a client wants something that isn’t in the packages?
